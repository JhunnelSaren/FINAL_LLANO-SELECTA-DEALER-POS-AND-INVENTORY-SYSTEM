# PROJECT: LLANO SELECTA DEALER POS AND INVENTORY SYSTEM

Act as a senior full-stack developer, database designer, QA engineer, and UI/UX designer.

Build a complete, functional, user-friendly web application named **Llano Selecta Dealer POS and Inventory System** for a Selecta ice cream dealer operating in Valencia City, Bukidnon, San Fernando, and Cabanglasan, Philippines.

The main goal is to replace the dealer's scattered Excel records and manual tracking with one organized system while keeping the interface extremely simple for non-technical staff.

This must be a working system with persistent database records, functioning buttons and forms, correct inventory calculations, usable reports, and a complete end-to-end workflow. Do not stop at a design, prototype, or frontend mockup.

## 1. FIRST: INSPECT THE EXISTING PROJECT

Before changing any code:

1. Inspect the existing workspace, folders, source files, dependencies, and database.
2. Look for the Excel booking template, sample barcode photographs, and other business documents if they are available inside the workspace.
3. If an Excel file is found, inspect its worksheets, columns, sample data, formulas, and booking layout. Use it as a reference for the booking entry, import, and export functions. Never overwrite the original file.
4. If a freezer barcode photograph is available, use it to understand the evidence the business needs to record. Do not invent details that cannot be read.
5. If these reference files are missing, continue implementation using the business rules in this prompt. Create an importable Excel booking template and document the assumptions.

Prepare a concise implementation plan and database design, then proceed with implementation. Do not stop after presenting the plan. Do not ask unnecessary questions; make reasonable assumptions and put changeable business rules in Settings.

## 2. TECHNOLOGY AND PROJECT STRUCTURE

Preserve the existing project's working technology if it already has a suitable implementation.

If the workspace is empty, use a simple, maintainable architecture:

- Frontend: React, TypeScript, and Vite.
- UI: Tailwind CSS with reusable components and accessible form controls.
- Backend: Node.js and Express with TypeScript.
- Database: Prisma ORM and SQLite for local development.
- Authentication: secure password hashing and protected sessions.
- File uploads: securely store barcode evidence photographs and relevant document attachments, with their paths and metadata recorded in the database.
- Testing: use an appropriate testing framework for business logic and API behavior.

Do not use localStorage as the primary database. Persistent records must survive browser refreshes, restarts, and logging out.

Keep the project easy to run in VS Code. Avoid unnecessary microservices, complicated infrastructure, and excessive dependencies. Document how to move to a production database if the dealer later needs a shared, hosted system.

Create a .env.example file, appropriate database migrations, seed/demo data, and a detailed README.md.

## 3. SIMPLE UI REQUIREMENTS

Use a clean, professional, modern business interface appropriate for a small Philippine ice cream dealership.

The interface must be easy enough for a new staff member to understand without lengthy training.

Use exactly five primary navigation items:

1. Dashboard
2. Orders & Delivery
3. Inventory
4. Stores & Freezers
5. Sales & Reports

Put Settings and Staff Management behind a small gear icon or a clearly labeled settings menu.

Do not create separate main navigation items for every small function. Group related functions inside each main module using tabs, dropdown menus, and contextual action buttons.

### Dashboard

Display a clear overview using readable cards and compact tables:

- Today's bookings and scheduled deliveries.
- Orders waiting for the Selecta bodega request.
- Stock expected to arrive.
- Products currently in stock and products below their minimum level.
- Deliveries in progress and deliveries awaiting reconciliation.
- Outstanding customer balances.
- Cash shortages, delivery discrepancies, and pending approvals.
- Freezers due for their monthly barcode verification.
- Current month's Selecta POS quota and progress.

Every dashboard card should open the relevant filtered records when clicked.

Avoid excessive charts, decorative animations, complicated graphs, and clutter.

### Interface design rules

- Use plain, understandable English labels.
- Use Philippine peso formatting, such as ₱1,250.00.
- Use Asia/Manila as the business timezone.
- Use large, readable action buttons.
- Use a clear search bar, date filters, route filters, and status filters.
- Use tables with only important columns and an obvious View/Edit action.
- Use confirmation dialogs for consequential actions.
- Display helpful validation messages beside incorrect fields.
- Clearly distinguish Draft, Pending, In Progress, Completed, Overdue, and Requires Attention.
- Make the delivery workflow usable on a mobile phone as well as a desktop computer.
- Every button, form, dropdown, filter, and export action must work.
- Do not leave placeholder links, dummy buttons, or nonfunctional sample actions.
- Do not display fake success messages when an operation fails.

## 4. CORE MODULE: ORDERS & DELIVERY

Place all booking, supplier-order tracking, receiving, preparation, delivery, and reconciliation processes inside this one main module.

Use these sub-tabs:

- Bookings
- Bodega Order
- Stock Receiving
- Prepare & Load
- Delivery
- Delivery Reconciliation

### A. Bookings

The booker visits stores and records each customer's requested products and quantities.

Each booking must contain:

- Unique booking number.
- Booking date and requested delivery date.
- Delivery area and route.
- Customer/store name.
- Booker responsible for the booking.
- Products, quantities, unit prices, and calculated totals.
- Notes or special delivery instructions.
- Booking status and timestamps.

Provide a simple booking form with a customer search, product search, quantity fields, and automatic total calculations.

Support multiple products per store and multiple stores per route.

Allow the owner to edit or reopen bookings only when appropriate and record significant changes in the audit log.

### Booking schedule

Use the following defaults, but make them editable in Settings:

- Monday: Valencia–San Fernando booking route.
- Tuesday: Cabanglasan booking route.

Support Batangan, San Isidro, Lumabayo, Valencia City, San Fernando, and the different Cabanglasan delivery areas through configurable customer locations and route assignments.

Allow the owner to change the schedule when staff, holidays, stock arrivals, or other circumstances require it.

### Booking documents

Provide:

- A printable booking sheet that resembles a useful paper order form.
- Excel export using customer names, product names, quantities, and totals.
- Excel import with column mapping and validation.
- A consolidated booking summary showing the quantity of each product required across all stores.
- A per-store booking sheet for the booker and owner.

Show validation errors for unknown products, duplicate records, invalid quantities, and unmatched customers. Do not silently import incorrect data.

### B. Bodega Order

Llano already uses the official Selecta POS to order products from the Selecta bodega.

The new application must not pretend to replace or directly integrate with this external POS.

Provide a workflow for the owner to:

1. Review consolidated bookings.
2. Review the total quantities needed.
3. Record that the order has been entered in the official Selecta POS.
4. Record the official reference number, order date, expected arrival date, and staff responsible.
5. Track the supplier order status.

Allow the owner to compare requested quantities against quantities actually received.

Do not assume a supplier order has been placed merely because a record exists in Llano's application.

### C. Stock Receiving

When the Selecta truck panel arrives, the checker records the actual delivery from the supplier.

Capture:

- Linked supplier order.
- Supplier delivery or receipt reference.
- Date and time received.
- Truck plate number.
- Name of the receiving staff member.
- Product quantities expected and quantities physically received.
- Damaged, melted, missing, or otherwise unacceptable items.
- Notes and photograph of the signed supplier receipt when available.

Use a simple item-by-item receiving checklist.

Allow the checker to accept the actual quantity received instead of automatically accepting the ordered quantity.

Record the supplier receipt as a completed or partially received transaction.

Good products enter saleable inventory. Damaged or questionable products must be tracked separately as non-saleable stock.

Never increase inventory simply because an order was requested.

### D. Prepare & Load

After receiving stock, the staff prepares each store's delivery.

Provide a preparation screen that shows:

- Store name.
- Route and delivery date.
- Booking number.
- Requested quantities.
- Available inventory.
- Quantities allocated, picked, and loaded.
- Unavailable or short quantities.

Calculate consolidated picking totals so the team knows how much of each product to retrieve.

Provide printable or downloadable box labels containing the store name, route, delivery date, and a useful summary of the box contents.

Support the Bongo vehicle and a flexible delivery crew consisting of one driver and two loaders. Allow a backride or replacement staff member where needed.

Record the actual driver, helpers, receipt collector, and loading staff assigned to each trip.

Prevent dispatch when stock is unavailable unless the owner explicitly authorizes the exception.

Track dispatched stock as in transit until the delivery is reconciled.

### E. Delivery

A delivery run can contain multiple stores.

For every store, display the original booking, loaded products, and a simple actual-delivery entry form.

Record:

- Ordered quantity.
- Loaded quantity.
- Actual quantity delivered and accepted.
- Missing or not-delivered quantity.
- BO or melted quantity refused by the store and brought back.
- Other approved adjustments.
- Final amount to collect.
- Payment received, payment method, and outstanding balance.
- Name of the delivery staff and recipient.
- Notes or evidence for discrepancies.

The staff must be able to complete the delivery of one store and continue to the next store without losing the current progress.

Use simple status buttons such as Start Delivery, Save Store Result, Mark Delivered, and Finish Route.

Allow partial delivery, rescheduling, and documented exceptions.

### F. Delivery Reconciliation

When the vehicle returns, compare all store delivery records with the original route plan.

Show:

- Total products loaded.
- Total products delivered and accepted.
- Products not delivered but physically returned intact.
- BO/melted products returned.
- Items missing and not physically accounted for.
- Amount expected from customers.
- Cash collected.
- GCash payments.
- Check payments by clearing status.
- Outstanding customer balances.
- Total discrepancy and its explanation.

Require unresolved stock or collection discrepancies to be reviewed before final route closure. Permit an authorized owner to approve an exception with a recorded reason.

## 5. CORE MODULE: INVENTORY

This is the dealer's internal inventory, separate from the official Selecta POS.

Provide simple sub-tabs:

- Stock on Hand
- Stock Movements
- Damaged / BO
- Low Stock

Each product should have:

- Unique product ID or SKU.
- Selecta product name.
- Barcode when available.
- Category.
- Unit of measure.
- Current selling price.
- Cost or other valuation fields when available.
- Minimum-stock threshold.
- Active/inactive status.

Maintain an auditable stock movement ledger.

Record each stock movement from supplier receiving, picking, dispatch, sales, returned intact items, BO returns, approved adjustments, and stock counts.

Keep saleable stock separate from damaged, melted, and quarantined stock.

For each BO return, record the product, quantity, reason, original delivery, store, date, and staff member. Require a documented disposition such as pending inspection, approved write-off, or another authorized outcome.

A BO return must never automatically become saleable inventory.

When intact products are not delivered, return them to stock only after they are physically verified. If products cannot be accounted for, record a shortage for investigation instead of silently adding stock back.

Prevent negative stock by default. If the owner permits an exceptional adjustment, record the reason and user.

Add:

- Manual physical stock counts.
- Approved inventory adjustments.
- Low-stock warnings.
- Inventory valuation where the necessary costs are available.
- Optional batch and expiry-date tracking when supplier data supports it.
- Stock balance and movement reports.
- Excel export.

All stock values must be derived from database records and transactions, not hardcoded demo values.

## 6. CORE MODULE: STORES & FREEZERS

Use two simple tabs:

- Stores
- Freezers & Monthly Verification

### A. Stores

Maintain a customer/store directory with:

- Store/customer code.
- Store name.
- Contact person and phone.
- Municipality, barangay, area, and delivery route.
- Delivery address or directions.
- Active/inactive status.
- Notes.
- Outstanding balance summary.
- Assigned freezer, when applicable.

Include a fast search by store name, area, and customer code.

Show the store's booking history, delivery history, receipts, payments, and BO records from its profile.

Prevent accidental creation of duplicate stores.

### B. Freezers & Monthly Verification

Selecta owns the freezers provided to stores. Track the equipment as Selecta-owned assets and never automatically treat them as dealer-owned inventory or sold products.

Each freezer record should include:

- Unique freezer ID.
- Barcode or serial number.
- Assigned store.
- Location.
- Assignment and installation dates.
- Status: Active, Under Maintenance, Returned, or Unassigned.
- Date last verified.
- Next monthly verification due date.
- Verification history and photo evidence.

Track the freezer-program payment separately:

- Total amount: ₱10,000.
- Product allocation: ₱9,000.
- Processing fee: ₱1,000.

Record the transaction date, customer, payment method, reference, and receipt. Make the accounting classification configurable rather than assuming the amount is a refundable deposit.

### Monthly barcode verification

Staff must continue using their existing Timemark app and cellphone to perform the actual barcode scan and required photo capture.

Do not claim integration with Timemark unless an actual, documented and authorized integration becomes available.

After the Timemark scan, staff can open the freezer record in Llano's system and record:

- Verification month.
- Date and time scanned.
- Scanned barcode or serial number.
- Staff member who performed the scan.
- Uploaded photo evidence.
- Notes and verification status.

Allow the owner to review or reject incomplete evidence.

Calculate the next monthly due date automatically and display Due Soon, Due, Completed, or Overdue.

Prevent duplicate monthly records for the same freezer and verification period unless an authorized correction is made.

Provide a printable/exportable monthly compliance report.

## 7. CORE MODULE: SALES & REPORTS

Group receipts, payment collection, reconciliation, quota tracking, and reporting inside this module.

### A. Final customer bill

Customer charges must reflect the actual products delivered and accepted.

For each delivery line, keep separate records for ordered quantity, loaded quantity, accepted quantity, missing quantity, and BO/returned quantity.

Use this billing rule:

Billable product amount = actual saleable quantity accepted by the store × the applicable unit selling price.

Net amount due = billable product amount + other approved charges − approved discounts or credits + any explicitly included prior balance.

A product that was never delivered is not charged to the customer.

A BO product that is refused and returned must not be charged as an accepted sale. If the original bill already charged for that item, post a separate credit adjustment rather than deducting the same item twice.

Keep the original booking and all approved changes traceable. A correction must never silently overwrite the original transaction.

Make the final amount due visible before the receipt is finalized.

### B. Receipts and payments

Every delivery must generate a uniquely numbered customer receipt or delivery invoice.

Include:

- Receipt number and date.
- Customer and store details.
- Route and related booking.
- Product names, unit prices, quantities, and line amounts.
- Missing and returned quantities where applicable.
- Approved credits or deductions.
- Final net amount due.
- Payments received and methods.
- Outstanding balance.
- Staff/collector name.
- Customer acknowledgment or signature area.

Support Cash, GCash, Bank Transfer, and Check.

Capture appropriate reference information, such as a GCash transaction reference or a check number and bank.

Checks must support statuses such as Pending, Cleared, and Bounced. Do not treat pending checks as cleared funds.

Support partial payments and unpaid balances.

Prevent the same payment reference from being accidentally recorded twice. Allow authorized corrections with an audit trail.

Provide a receipt that can be printed or saved as a PDF using a working print layout.

### C. Daily collection reconciliation

At the end of a delivery run or workday, show:

- Total finalized customer bills.
- Cash expected.
- Cash actually counted.
- GCash collections.
- Bank transfer collections.
- Checks received, separated by status.
- Total collections by method.
- Customer balances still outstanding.
- Overages or shortages.

Calculate:

Cash variance = actual cash counted − expected cash to be collected in cash.

For example, if cash expected is ₱20,000 but the actual cash counted is ₱19,000, show a ₱1,000 cash shortage.

Do not change the receipt total merely to make the money match.

Require the user to record a reason for any difference. Restrict approval and final closure of significant discrepancies to the owner or an authorized user.

Produce a collection summary by delivery route, collection date, collector, and payment method.

### D. Monthly Selecta POS quota

Create a simple monthly quota page.

The default example target is ₱1,000,000, but the owner must be able to change the target for each month.

Store the following separately:

- Monthly sales target.
- Actual sales figures from the official Selecta POS.
- Date the official POS figure was recorded.
- Reference or supporting note.
- Staff member who entered or verified the figure.
- Remaining amount to reach the target.
- Percentage achieved.

Allow manual daily or monthly updates based on the official Selecta POS, with a history of previous entries or corrections.

Use clearly labeled fields and totals. Do not assume that internal delivery totals automatically equal official Selecta quota figures.

Make the quota dashboard understandable at a glance.

### E. Reports

Include these reports under simple tabs or a Reports dropdown:

1. Daily and monthly sales.
2. Booking and delivery fulfillment.
3. Inventory balance and stock movement.
4. Missing, damaged, and BO products.
5. Customer payments and outstanding balances.
6. Cash collection and discrepancies.
7. Monthly Selecta POS quota.
8. Freezer monthly verification compliance.
9. Staff and delivery-run summaries.

Support date, route, store, and staff filters where relevant.

Allow printing and Excel export. Ensure report totals match the underlying saved transactions and do not double-count revised or credited invoices.

## 10. REQUIRED END-TO-END WORKFLOW

Implement and verify this complete example:

1. The booker creates a Monday or Tuesday booking with several stores and different product quantities.
2. The booking produces a consolidated product summary and printable booking sheets.
3. The owner marks that the supplier request was submitted through the official Selecta POS and records its reference.
4. The truck arrives. The checker records actual good and damaged quantities, the truck plate, receiving date, and receiver.
5. The system updates inventory and identifies insufficient stock.
6. The team prepares and labels store-specific boxes and assigns the actual driver and two loaders, or substitute staff.
7. The delivery team records actual accepted quantities and any missing or BO products at each stop.
8. The application calculates the correct final bill without double-deducting a BO credit or charging for undelivered items.
9. The collector records cash, GCash, bank transfer, or checks, with any remaining customer balance.
10. At the end of the run, the owner compares expected cash with actual cash and investigates discrepancies.
11. Reconciliation updates inventory and records BO returns, intact returned stock, shortages, and any approved adjustments.
12. The owner reviews the sales reports, official POS quota, staff activity, and monthly freezer verification compliance.

The sample delivery calendar should permit the business to use its usual pattern: after an example Wednesday stock arrival, Cabanglasan deliveries on Thursday and Valencia–San Fernando deliveries on Friday. These schedules must remain editable, not permanently hardcoded.

## 11. IMPORTANT ACCOUNTING AND INVENTORY SAFEGUARDS

Never silently change transaction totals to conceal a shortage.

Never deduct a BO product twice.

Never sell returned damaged products without an explicit authorized disposition.

Never count an uncollected or uncleared payment as settled cash.

Never assume ordered quantities equal received quantities or delivered quantities.

Never increase available stock for products that are still missing or in transit.

Never count internal records and official Selecta POS figures together as though they were two independent sources of sales.

Ensure all final amounts, balances, and stock counts are calculated from transaction details on the server and stored consistently.

Use Philippine peso formatting throughout.
